# Forklift Operations Manual

## Pre-Shift Inspection
Every forklift operator must complete a pre-shift inspection checklist before the first use of the day. The checklist covers tire condition, fork straightness, hydraulic leaks, horn function, backup alarm, seatbelt condition, and mast chain tension. Any defect found during inspection must be logged on the inspection sheet and reported to the shift supervisor before the forklift is used. A forklift with a safety-critical defect, such as a non-functioning brake or a leaking hydraulic line, must be tagged out and removed from service immediately. Inspection sheets are collected at the end of each shift and reviewed weekly by the maintenance lead to spot recurring issues before they become breakdowns. Operators who skip the pre-shift inspection twice in a rolling 90-day period are required to repeat the internal certification course.

## Operating Rules
Forklifts must not exceed 8 km/h in warehouse aisles and 5 km/h near pedestrian crossings. Operators must sound the horn at every blind corner and intersection. Loads must be tilted back and carried low, no higher than 15 cm off the ground, while traveling. Riders other than the operator are never permitted on the forklift, including on the forks or the load. Operators must hold a valid internal forklift certification, renewed every three years, and must wear a seatbelt whenever the forklift is in motion. Forklifts must be parked with forks fully lowered, the parking brake engaged, and the key removed whenever left unattended, even for a short break. Racing, stunt driving, and using the forklift to push pallets without engaging the forks are all strictly prohibited.

## Pedestrian Zones
Marked pedestrian walkways take priority over forklift traffic at all crossings. Forklifts must come to a complete stop at every marked crossing and confirm the walkway is clear before proceeding. Pedestrians are not permitted to walk under a raised load or between a forklift and a rack upright. Convex mirrors are installed at blind corners to improve visibility for both operators and pedestrians. High-visibility vests are mandatory for all pedestrians on the warehouse floor, and forklift operators are trained to make eye contact or use the horn to confirm a pedestrian has seen them before proceeding through a shared aisle.

## Battery Charging
Electric forklift batteries must be charged only in the designated charging bay, which has eyewash stations and spill containment installed. Operators must wear safety glasses and gloves when connecting or disconnecting charging cables. Charging areas are no-smoking, no-open-flame zones due to hydrogen off-gassing during charging. A battery showing swelling, cracking, or excessive corrosion on the terminals must be reported and taken out of service. Batteries must be allowed to cool for at least thirty minutes after a shift before charging begins, and charging cables must be fully seated before the charger is switched on to avoid arcing at the connector.

## Incident Reporting
Any forklift collision, near-miss, tip-over, or load drop must be reported to the shift supervisor within one hour, regardless of whether damage or injury occurred. A written incident report is required within 24 hours. Forklifts involved in a tip-over or collision with a structure must be inspected by maintenance before returning to service, and the operator must complete a refresher training session before operating a forklift again. Repeated incidents involving the same operator within a twelve-month period trigger a formal review by the site safety committee, which may result in additional training, restricted duties, or suspension of forklift privileges.
