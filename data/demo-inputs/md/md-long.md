# Crane Operations Manual

## Pre-Operation Checks
Before any lift, the crane operator inspects the hoist line for fraying, confirms the load chart posted in the cab matches the current boom configuration, and tests the upper and lower limit switches. Any defect found during the pre-operation check must be logged and reported to the crane maintenance coordinator before the crane is used for a lift. A crane with a safety-critical defect, such as a damaged hook latch or a non-functioning limit switch, is tagged out of service immediately. The operator also confirms outrigger pads are set on stable, level ground before extending the boom, since an unstable base is one of the leading causes of tip-over incidents on mobile cranes.

## Rigging Standards
Only rigging hardware rated for the intended load and inspected within the last 12 months may be used. Slings must be selected based on the load weight, center of gravity, and the angle of the lift, never estimated by eye. Riggers must attach a tag line to any load that could rotate or swing once it clears the ground, and the tag line must be long enough to keep the rigger well outside the load's swing radius. Hooks must have a functioning safety latch, and any sling showing cuts, abrasion, or crushed fibers is removed from service on the spot rather than used for one more lift.

## Load Charts
The crane's rated capacity changes with boom angle, boom length, and counterweight configuration, so operators must consult the load chart for every new lift rather than relying on the capacity used for a previous lift. Lifts within 90 percent of rated capacity require a second qualified person to confirm the load weight and chart reading before the lift proceeds. Any lift where the load weight is uncertain is treated as a critical lift and requires a written lift plan reviewed by the site safety officer beforehand.

## Signal Communication
Only one designated signal person directs the crane operator during a lift, using standard hand signals or a two-way radio with a clear channel. If radio contact is lost during a lift already in progress, the operator holds the load in place until visual signal contact is reestablished rather than guessing at the next move. Before the lift begins, the operator and signal person confirm they are using the same signal set and agree on an emergency stop signal that overrides all other instructions.

## Exclusion Zones
The area beneath and around a suspended load is a strict exclusion zone marked with cones or barrier tape before the lift begins. No one is permitted to walk under a suspended load for any reason, including to retrieve dropped tools. The exclusion zone radius is set to at least the maximum boom length to account for a possible boom or line failure. Once the load is set down and the rigging is slack, the signal person confirms the area is clear before the exclusion zone barriers are removed and normal foot traffic resumes.
