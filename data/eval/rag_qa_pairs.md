# RAG Evaluation Question Set

15 questions per document (5 easy / 5 medium / 5 hard) — 45 total.
Difficulty definitions used:
- **Easy**: single fact, directly stated, one location in the doc.
- **Medium**: requires reading a short paragraph/table and light synthesis (a "why" or a list of items).
- **Hard**: requires cross-referencing multiple sections, comparing values across a table, or tracing a multi-step procedure/responsibility chain.

---

## Document 1: OPNL_B01_EN.pdf — "Operational Safety / Safety Instructions" (Rev 4_0, 7 Nov 2024)

### Easy

**Q1.** What is the minimum general lighting level (in lux) that must be established on and around the machinery?
**A1.** 300 lux. (Section 3.8)

**Q2.** What is the minimum age a passenger must be to operate the CrisBag® Self Bag Drop (SBD)?
**A2.** 15 years old, and they must have normal human strength. (Section 3.6.6)

**Q3.** What lighting level is required specifically at the workplaces (as opposed to general area lighting)?
**A3.** 500 lux. (Section 3.8)

**Q4.** What color and geometric shape represents a "Warning" sign category (Category W) in this document's safety sign system?
**A4.** A yellow triangle. (Section 6.3, Category W)

**Q5.** What revision of this document is current, and when was it issued?
**A5.** Revision 4_0, issued 7 November 2024. (Cover page / Section 1 Revisions)

### Medium

**Q6.** Above what free-fall height must safety precautions (e.g., scaffolding, railing, or a safety harness) be used when working at height?
**A6.** More than 2.0 metres of potential free fall. (Sections 3.1.2 and 3.6.3)

**Q7.** Name at least three of the five categories of documentation that must be kept in a CrisStore® module's log book.
**A7.** Any three of: completed maintenance forms proving scheduled maintenance was done; a record of faults noted during operation; documentation of all major repairs; documentation of tests following modifications or rebuilding; documentation of tests made during manufacture of the miniloader. (Section 3.4)

**Q8.** For how long is it considered acceptable to rely on a safety harness alone as an emergency fall-protection solution?
**A8.** For short-duration tasks, normally less than four man-hours. (Section 3.7.1)

**Q9.** Before persons may enter the fenced-in area around a CrisStore® miniloader, what two actions must be taken regarding the switch-disconnector?
**A9.** The switch-disconnector on the LCC Master must be turned to the '0/OFF' position to cut supply voltage to the miniloader motors, and it must then be locked with the entering person's personal padlock. (Section 3.6.2)

**Q10.** What two general categories of safety devices are used to secure the machinery's danger zones against unauthorized access?
**A10.** Fixed guards (such as fencing) and movable guards (such as doors) that are equipped with guard locking and interlocking devices where necessary. (Section 3.11)

### Hard

**Q11.** Compare the mandatory training for a general "Other operation" worker versus a worker in "Cleaning, maintenance and repair" according to the training matrix — which module is mandatory for both, and which additional modules become mandatory only for the maintenance role?
**A11.** "Operator training, module 1" is mandatory for both operation and cleaning/maintenance/repair roles. The cleaning/maintenance/repair role additionally requires "Controls training, module 1" and "Maintenance training, module 1" as mandatory, with "Operator training module 2," "Controls training module 2," and "Maintenance training module 2" as optional refreshers. (Section 5.1, training matrix)

**Q12.** According to the revision history, in which revision was CrisBag® Self Bag Drop (SBD) safety content first introduced, and which specific subsections were added at that time?
**A12.** Revision 3_0 (13 Nov 2023), which added the SBD content to section 3.6 along with new subsections 3.6.6, 3.6.7, 3.6.8, and 3.6.9. (Section 1, Revisions)

**Q13.** Walk through the complete four-step procedure required to restart the machinery after an emergency stop, including who is authorized to perform the key step.
**A13.** (1) Investigate the cause of the emergency stop and eliminate the hazard; (2) deactivate the local emergency stop; (3) reactivate the emergency stop relay, which requires a security key held only by an authorized person; (4) acknowledge the E-stop message in the control system. (Section 4.1)

**Q14.** During dismantling of the machinery, what must never be removed until the body of the machinery has been fully dismantled, and why does this matter given the other steps in the procedure?
**A14.** Supports or platforms must never be removed before the machinery body has been dismantled — this matters because the procedure requires removing parts top-down and keeping the machinery stable throughout, since instability and stored/potential energy are explicitly flagged hazards of the dismantling process. (Section 3.14)

**Q15.** The document lists two separate hazards tied to the CrisBag® SBD front door (sections 3.6.8 and 3.6.9). What are they, and what single precaution is given for both?
**A15.** Section 3.6.8 warns of crushing of hands and fingers because the front door closes automatically; section 3.6.9 warns that clothing or similar items may get caught in the drive pulley. Both sections give the identical mitigation instruction: keep a distance when the front door is closing.

## Document 2: OPNL_B02_EN.pdf — "Prerequisites for Access" (Rev 2:0, 13 Nov 2023)

### Easy

**Q1.** How many distinct person profiles does the document define for access to the system?
**A1.** Four: Exposed person, Operator (Normal operation), Control room operator, and Service technician. (Section 3.2)

**Q2.** What condition must be met before an "Exposed person" is allowed near the system at all?
**A2.** They must always be accompanied by a service technician who has permission to walk in and around the system. (Section 3.2.1)

**Q3.** Why is the "walk-over" identified as the preferred means of crossing the conveyor/CrisBag® system?
**A3.** Because a walkway above the equipment does not interrupt the transport flow, so the system does not need to be stopped. (Section 5.2)

**Q4.** What free width does the walk-through gate provide when opened to its 90-degree position?
**A4.** 700 mm. (Section 5.6)

**Q5.** What visual method is used to mark escape routes on the floor/platform, per section 4.1?
**A5.** Luminescent arrows or tape. (Section 4.1)

### Medium

**Q6.** How does a Control room operator's access to machinery with general guarding differ from a Service technician's?
**A6.** A control room operator must not access dangerous machinery equipped with general guarding (e.g. discharges, lifts, diverters, medium/high-speed lines) at all, whereas a service technician is permitted into that same guarded machinery once the energy supply has been disconnected. (Sections 3.3.3 and 3.3.4)

**Q7.** Which four training modules must a Service technician complete according to Table 3-1?
**A7.** Operator Training, Controls Training, Hardware Training, and Maintenance Training. (Table 3-1)

**Q8.** What is the purpose of the guard cover installed where there is passage below a conveyor or CrisBag® element?
**A8.** It prevents the person passing underneath from being able to reach into the machine. (Section 5.3)

**Q9.** Functionally, how does a "step-in" crossing differ from a "walk-through" crossing, beyond both requiring the CrisBag® line to stop?
**A9.** A step-in is a single CrisBag® element fitted with a stepping base and small steps on each side, used where other access options aren't possible due to building or equipment constraints. A walk-through instead uses a dedicated gate driven by an adjoining element that swings open 90 degrees (giving 700 mm of width) and can be used in both one- and two-level systems. (Sections 5.5 and 5.6)

**Q10.** How is separation maintained between operator escape routes and maintenance areas, and how can the separating doors be opened in an emergency?
**A10.** Fences or the building's own walls separate the operator access/egress routes from the maintenance areas, and the doors between them use a locking function built into the door handle so only skilled (or accompanied) personnel can pass — but these doors can always be opened from the maintenance side in the event of fire or other accidents. (Section 4.2)

### Hard

**Q11.** Using Table 3-1 and section 3.3.3, explain precisely why a Control room operator's access sits "between" an Operator's and a Service technician's — what specific restriction caps it below the Service technician level even though it covers more zones than the Operator?
**A11.** A Control room operator, after training, gains access to the danger area in addition to the restricted and traffic areas that an Operator is limited to — broader than an Operator. However, the Control room operator is explicitly barred from any machinery equipped with general guarding (discharges, lifts, diverters, medium/high-speed lines) regardless of training, while a Service technician can enter that exact guarded machinery once energy is disconnected. That specific carve-out is what caps the control room operator's access below the service technician's.

**Q12.** Cross-referencing the revision log and section 5.7: what new access-related topic was introduced in revision 2_0, and where must a reader go to find its full procedure?
**A12.** Revision 2_0 added text and a reference to "Exceptional access," noting that at some safety-guarded equipment it is necessary to remove the fencing to gain access — the full procedure for this is detailed in chapter B-3. (Section 1, Revisions; Section 5.7)

**Q13.** Trace the complete responsibility chain, start to finish, when an operator notices an error in the system.
**A13.** The operator is not permitted to intervene to fix the error themselves; they report it to the control room or a service technician (section 3.3.2). Only trained service technicians are allowed to actually eliminate errors on the CrisBag® system (section 5.9). The control room takes over responsibility for dispatching a service technician. If an emergency stop is involved, the system can only be reset after the service technician has found the cause and confirmed there is no danger in resuming automatic operation, after which it is reported back to the control room that the system has been returned to automatic operation (section 5.8).

**Q14.** What mechanical safety redundancy exists at a "step-in" crossing beyond the standard safe access control system, and what specific failure mode does it guard against?
**A14.** The step-in is additionally secured with a mechanical system that prevents totes from impacting a person walking through the element if a PLC failure occurs — i.e., it's a hardware backstop against a control-system (software/PLC) failure, not just an electronic interlock. (Section 5.5)

**Q15.** Synthesizing sections 3.2.2 and 3.3, explain why an Operator is restricted to established, wider access routes with walk-over bridges rather than using the step-in/walk-through units that cross directly through the CrisBag® line.
**A15.** Operators' access permission is explicitly limited to restricted tasks within the restricted and traffic areas, using routes designed to be wide with safe walk-over bridges — this is the access tier they receive after only "Operator Training." Step-ins and walk-throughs cross directly into/through the CrisBag® line itself, which is part of the zone reserved for personnel trained in Controls, Hardware, and/or Maintenance modules (control room operators and service technicians). Since operators are not trained or authorized for direct line-crossing access, the system design keeps them on the separated, wider routes instead.

## Document 3: Operating_instructions_CLV69x_Bar_code_scanners_en_IM0045360.pdf (SICK CLV69x, 134 pages)

### Easy

**Q1.** What is the CLV69x's stated intended use?
**A1.** It is an intelligent opto-electronic ID sensor used for automatic, fixed identification and decoding of bar codes on moving or stationary objects, sending the decoded data to a higher-level PLC for further processing. (Section 2.1)

**Q2.** What laser class does the CLV69x comply with?
**A2.** Laser class 2, per EN/IEC 60825-1. (Sections 2.7.1 and 12.1)

**Q3.** What is the device's enclosure (ingress protection) rating?
**A3.** IP 65. (Section 12.4)

**Q4.** What software is used to configure the device?
**A4.** SOPAS ET configuration software. (Section 12.3 and Chapter 7)

**Q5.** What is the housing color of the device?
**A5.** Light blue (RAL 5012). (Section 12.4)

### Medium

**Q6.** According to the laser safety chapter, how long can the human eye be briefly exposed to the beam without being considered at risk?
**A6.** Up to 0.25 seconds; longer exposure may damage the retina. (Section 2.7.1)

**Q7.** What data can potentially be lost when the device is simply switched off?
**A7.** Application-specific parameter sets that were only temporarily stored, the last reading result, and the daily operating hours counter. (Section 2.8)

**Q8.** What is the scanning frequency range and the reading distance range for the CLV690 Standard Density variant?
**A8.** Scanning frequency 400 Hz to 1200 Hz; reading distance 500 mm to 2100 mm. (Section 12.1)

**Q9.** What key precaution does the manual give for cleaning the reading window, and why does it matter?
**A9.** Clean the reading window only when wet, using a mild, powder-free cleaning agent, and avoid any motion that could scratch or abrade it — because scratches or streaks reduce reading performance. (Section 9.2.1)

**Q10.** What is the supply voltage range for a device without heating versus one with heating?
**A10.** Without heating: 18 V DC to 30 V DC; with heating: 21.6 V DC to 28.8 V DC. (Section 12.4)

### Hard

**Q11.** Compare the reading distance and code resolution ranges across the Standard, Low Density, and High Density variants, and explain the trade-off the numbers reveal.
**A11.** Standard (CLV690): 500–2100 mm distance, 0.25–1.0 mm resolution. Low Density (CLV691): 500–2200 mm distance (the longest range), 0.35–1.2 mm resolution (the coarsest). High Density (CLV692): 400–1600 mm distance (the shortest range), 0.17–0.4 mm resolution (the finest). The pattern shows a direct trade-off: the variant tuned for the longest reading distance has the coarsest achievable resolution, while the variant capable of the finest resolution only works at shorter range. (Section 12.1)

**Q12.** Explain the interaction between the "laser timeout" feature and PSDI reading modes — under which specific operating conditions is the timeout setting rendered ineffective even if activated?
**A12.** Laser timeout is meant to switch off the laser diode automatically if the trigger pulse stops for too long (e.g., a stopped conveyor). However, the laser diode is permanently or repeatedly switched on regardless during "Percentage Evaluation" and "Auto Setup" modes, and during reading operation in the PSDI types "Auto pulse" (adjustable duty cycle) or "free." In all of these states, an activated timeout has no effect. (Section 2.7.1)

**Q13.** Per the Interfaces technical data, which cloning plugs enable serial (RS-232/RS-422/-485) communication versus which enable Ethernet, and what protocols does the Ethernet interface support?
**A13.** Serial communication requires cloning plugs 2062450, 2062452, 2062453, or 2062454. Ethernet requires cloning plugs 2062452, 2074708, or 2074710, and supports TCP/IP, EtherNet/IP, and PROFINET (the latter optionally via an external CDF 600-22xx fieldbus module, including a PROFINET DualPort option). (Section 12.3)

**Q14.** Trace the full safety-conscious cleaning sequence for the reading window, including the laser precaution and the differing methods for glass versus plastic lenses.
**A14.** First, switch off the device for the duration of cleaning, or if that isn't possible, wear laser protection goggles rated for the device's wavelength. For a glass lens: remove dust with a soft, clean brush, then if needed clean further with a clean, damp, lint-free cloth and a mild anti-static glass cleaner. For a plastic lens: skip dry brushing and clean only with a clean, damp, lint-free cloth plus the same mild anti-static glass cleaner. If the window is scratched, cracked, or broken, it must be replaced by SICK Service rather than cleaned. (Section 9.2.1)

**Q15.** Synthesizing sections 2.2 (Improper use) and 2.4 (Limitation of liability), describe a scenario in which both of SICK's liability-exclusion clauses would apply to the same incident simultaneously.
**A15.** If a customer both (a) uses the device outside its intended purpose or technical specification — e.g., installing it in an explosion-hazardous or corrosive environment, which section 2.2 explicitly calls out as improper use — and (b) has also made an unauthorized conversion or technical modification to the device, then both exclusion grounds in section 2.4 (incorrect use, and unauthorized conversions/technical modifications) would apply to the same incident, compounding SICK AG's disclaimer of liability.

### Notes for test setup
- Page/section references above point to the source location for verification but are not required in a model's answer unless your grading rubric asks for citations.
- "Hard" questions are intentionally designed so the answer cannot be found in one contiguous passage — a model that only does single-chunk retrieval is likely to under-perform on these, which makes them useful for evaluating multi-hop RAG retrieval.
